## Can we switch to the blogger's recipe AND use their picture?

**We can switch to/adapt the cooking method, but their picture is a separate copyrighted work.** A publicly readable recipe page does not grant a free license to its photograph or to the author's original written instructions.

- **With an appropriate image license or permission:** We can store the photo in Kitchen Buddy, follow its conditions, and link/credit the source as required. Under some licenses, a link back and an indication of cropping are required. We can produce our own original step-by-step wording from the cooking method and maintain the original recipe attribution.
- **When the website allows one photo only to promote a linked original recipe:** That generally means **a photo plus a link to their full recipe**, not bundling their photograph beside our own full ingredients and instructions. We'd need extra permission for that other use.
- **Without image rights:** We can safely **link to the original recipe page** for photo viewing, but should not embed or copy the image into a publicly hosted Kitchen Buddy app. Another option is taking a photo ourselves after cooking the recipe.
- **Personal offline cooking:** Privately saving a recipe for personal reference is different from publishing or redistributing its photo in a GitHub website. Don't treat a private-use assumption as a blanket right to republish.

**Release status:** All four are verified recipe-page references, not new installed recipes or licensed app image files. Routine photo selection no longer requires the user's separate approval; the assistant must check accuracy and photo rights. Any actual recipe changes require testing before merging, and deployment still needs user authorization.
